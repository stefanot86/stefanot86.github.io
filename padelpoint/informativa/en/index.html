PadelPoint — Privacy policy

Last updated 15 October 2026

In short: the data you put into PadelPoint — matches, players, photos — stays on your phone. The only thing that uses the internet is a Google AdMob advertising banner, at the bottom of the home screen and of the statistics. There are no accounts and no analytics tools.

Who is writing this

PadelPoint is developed by Stefano Toma, an independent developer. No other person, company or service is involved in handling the data you put in. The only outside service is Google's advertising service, described below.

What you put into the app

Everything the app keeps is something you gave it while scoring a match:

  • the names of the players and of the two pairs, if you choose to type them — you may skip them, and the app will call them "Player 1", "Player 2" and so on;
  • one photo per player, if you choose to add one;
  • the side of the court each player usually plays on;
  • the matches: start and end date and time, the rules you chose, the points in the order they were awarded and at what time, and the score that follows from them;
  • your preferences: spoken score on or off, beginner explanations, preferred rules, language.

There is nothing else. The app does not ask for your location, does not read your contacts, does not look at the other photos on your phone, and does not know who you are. For advertising, see its own section.

Where that data lives

In the app's own private storage, inside the phone: the match database and the photo folder. Android reserves that area for the app, and other apps cannot read it. Nothing else sees it, and there is no server of ours for it to be sent to.

What is not there

  • One permission only, internet access, and it is used only by the advertising banner. No part of the app written by us opens connections, and there is no server of ours.
  • No account and no sign-up.
  • No analytics, no usage measurement, no crash reporting.
  • No selling or sharing of the data you put in: matches, names and photos are not sent to anyone.

Advertising

At the bottom of the home screen and of the statistics there is a Google AdMob banner. It never appears during a match. To show it, the Google component included in the app talks to Google's servers and uses:

  • the phone's advertising ID, an identifier you can reset or delete;
  • the IP address, from which an approximate location is inferred (city or region, not the precise spot);
  • technical information about the phone: model, Android version, language;
  • interactions with the ad: whether it was shown and whether you tapped it.

They are used to choose and show the ad, measure its results and prevent fraud, and may be used to show ads based on your interests. Google handles them under its own rules: how Google uses information from apps that use its services. We do not receive or see them: AdMob only shows us aggregate figures, such as how many times the banner was seen.

You can reset or delete the advertising ID in Android's settings, under Privacy → Ads (the path varies a little between phones). The banner keeps appearing, but no longer tied to your interests.

The other three cases where something leaves the phone

We would rather say them than let you find them out.

  • Android's own backup. The app lets Android include it in the phone's automatic backup: a copy of the matches and photos ends up in your Google account backup, so that your data comes back when you change phone. That copy is yours and Google keeps it under its own terms; we have no way of seeing it. Android's settings let you turn the backup off or delete it.
  • The file you export yourself. The app can save a file with all your matches, photos and players, to keep a copy or move it to another phone. Where that file goes is your choice: if you send it through a chat or to an online service, the data leaves the phone because you decided so, and from that moment the terms of whoever receives it apply.
  • The score said out loud. If you turn the spoken score on, the score sentence — which may contain the pair names you typed — is handed to the phone's text-to-speech engine, which is another app, usually Google's. Some engines use the network for their better voices, under the terms of whoever makes them. If you would rather avoid that, leave the spoken score off: it is off by default, and only the settings turn it on.

Children

The app is not aimed specifically at children and collects no data on its own, so none from children either. The banner comes from Google AdMob, which applies its own rules to ad content. If a parent types a child's name as a player, that name stays on the phone like any other.

Deleting your data

  • A match can be deleted from the app, on its detail screen in the history.
  • Uninstalling the app makes Android delete its whole private storage: matches, players and photos go away with the app.
  • The copy in your Google backup is deleted from your Google account's backup settings, because that copy lives there and not on the phone.
  • Advertising data is kept by Google: you can delete the advertising ID in Android's settings, and manage your Google account's data at myaccount.google.com.

If this policy changes

The date at the top says when it was last written. If a future version of the app did something different with your data, this page would say so first, and the previous version would remain in the project's history.
